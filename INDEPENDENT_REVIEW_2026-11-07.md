# Independent scientific assessment of ThermalStorageBench (RP4)

7 November 2026

**Keep the 5 mm tank and 0.5 mm PCM datasets. I would delay submission of the combined benchmark, but I would not commission a finer production campaign.** The evidence supports using these runs as versioned numerical targets for surrogate modeling. The most consequential unresolved question is whether models that score similarly against production CFD still score similarly against the available alternative solutions. That question can be tested largely with work already done.

Two findings materially change how I would write the paper. First, the implemented tank “energy” and “outlet temperature” scores are proxies for the corresponding engineering quantities. Second, there is published prior work on both 2D tank-field prediction and open CFD data for latent storage. The strongest contribution is the combination of reusable transient fields, design-level evaluation, engineering observables, and documented numerical sensitivity.

### Tank numerical reliability

The four matched 5–2.5 mm comparisons in §5.2 give field differences of 0.34%, 0.60%, 1.70%, and 2.76% of ΔT. That is a useful measured range **for those four designs and that aggregation**. It is neither a pointwise bound nor an uncertainty interval. The 10 mm results do not establish monotonic convergence. Three of the four fine comparisons are laminar, and the sole SST mesh comparison is not either flagged SST case. The four designs cannot identify the upper tail across 588 SST runs.

- **T0243 gives the clearest evidence of a mesh effect.** The field difference grows from 0.41% to 2.49% across successive quarters, concentrates near the thermocline, and substantially exceeds the ≤0.11% inlet-perturbation response. Its energy difference of 0.96% is borderline under the study’s own 0.5% agreement criterion. The abstract should not say that every integral quantity passed. A moving interface can generate this pattern through displacement, broadening, or both. Comparing front position and thickness separately would distinguish those mechanisms.
- **T0551 gives evidence of unsteady sensitivity but no decomposition of error.** A 1.85% response to a small inlet perturbation does not explain a specified fraction of the 2.76% mesh response. The disturbances act differently, and their field differences need not align. Its early-window 3.03% also matters. T0408 similarly crosses the outlet agreement threshold during breakthrough despite passing on its whole-run average.
- **T0211 is a genuine warning about production settings.** Four outer correctors and halving maxCo each move the field roughly 7.3–7.4% from production, versus 0.47% for the inlet perturbation. The two alternatives still differ by 2.49%, borderline under the chosen criteria. This establishes numerical-setting dependence. It does not isolate PIMPLE iteration error from temporal truncation or prove either alternative is converged. More outer iterations improve within-step coupling, while smaller steps also make that coupling easier. Interface motion can amplify both effects. Outlet disagreement reaches 5.1% around breakthrough even though stored energy differs by under 0.5%. [T0211 comparison](verification_data/t0211x.json)
- **T0052 is scientifically important despite missing the first classification rule.** Its n4 field difference is 6.77%, inlet perturbation 1.68%, and outlet difference 11.35% during the event. Keep the original zero-of-four result and explicitly identify the retrospective flag. There is less causal evidence here than for T0211 because the table lacks the corresponding smaller-step comparison. [T0052 comparison](verification_data/regime_T0052_sst.json)
- **The neighbor study supports retention, with limited prevalence information.** Zero affected among ten prospectively selected, mode- and Ri-stratified runs is evidence against a ubiquitous problem in the sampled regime. The quoted 30.8% upper endpoint is the exact two-sided 95% binomial result for zero of ten independent, identically sampled trials. One hash-selected run per stratum and deliberate mode balancing give a different sampling design. Use the interval as an illustrative small-sample limit. Pooling the deliberately chosen index case, nearest neighbors, and stratified sample into “2/15” gives a descriptive count only. Also correct §5.4’s “12 of 14 … ≤0.05%”: T0156 alone has n4 = 1.80%. The ten prospective cases are the clean basis for the ≤0.05% statement.

The 192 closure pairs are an unusually valuable part of the project. The reported median field difference is 3.9%, reaching 17.8%, with substantially stronger association with Ri than Re (§5.5). These pairs measure sensitivity to modeling choice. They cannot determine which closure better represents a physical tank. With 38.5% of estimated wall cell-snapshots in the buffer layer and maxima up to 113, the local SST wall treatment deserves explicit documentation. State the wall functions, inlet turbulence specification, and outlet/backflow thermal conditions. The earlier faulty outlet in the abandoned subset does not establish a fault in the final batch. Conversely, matching a rerun or perturbing the inlet does not independently validate the production outlet condition.

**Tank rerun decision:** retain both flags and publish both alternative solutions. No family-wide rerun is justified. If selecting a *replacement* for either label becomes necessary, test n4 **and** maxCo 0.25 together on T0211 first, with matched physical times, actual step histories, pressure/continuity diagnostics, and event-window metrics. Add the smaller-step test for T0052 if its reference choice affects a reported conclusion. Until then the evidence supports alternate-reference scoring more strongly than it supports replacing production with n4.

### PCM verification and validation

For a 2:1 refinement ratio, let \(C,M,F\) denote coarse, medium and fine melt times. Under the single-leading-power assumption, \(p=\log_2[(M-C)/(F-M)]\) and \(t_\mathrm{ext}=F+(F-M)/(2^p-1)\). Applying those equations to §11.1 gives:

| Case | Independent calculation | Defensible interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| G131, 4200 / 4536 / 4746 s | Ratio \(336/210=1.6\), \(p=0.6781\), extrapolation 5096 s. With safety factor 1.25, medium GCI \(=1.25(560)/4536=15.43\%\), fine GCI \(=1.25(350)/4746=9.22\%\). | The arithmetic checks. Medium t99 is 11.0% below the extrapolation, but only 4.4% below the actual fine result, using the comparison target as denominator. The 11% includes an inferred tail. |
| G301, 3600 / 3920 / 4200 s | Ratio \(320/280=8/7\), \(p=0.1926\). The algebraic extrapolation is 6160 s. Medium is 6.67% below the observed fine result. | Low order makes extrapolation unstable. Withholding the GCI is justified. The roughly 7% is a two-grid difference. |
| P0021 | Nonmonotonic according to §11.1. | The stated monotonic formula cannot support a GCI. Its two-grid field discrepancy remains informative. |

Three monotonic points fit a power law by construction. G131’s accepted order and refinement ratio make the estimate reportable; they do not independently demonstrate an asymptotic range. Report time-step controls, event interpolation, t99 sampling resolution and pressure tolerances across levels. A mesh-dependent time step can fold temporal error into the apparent spatial order. Distinguish t99, the 0.995 stopping criterion, and experimental “full melt.” The 15.43% applies to **one scalar, one configuration**, not to temperatures, front positions, other materials, or a dataset-wide uncertainty distribution.

All six spot checks melt later on the fine grid, by 1.0–8.7%, with a median 4.9%. That is directional evidence across the sampled configurations. The field differences of 4.2–7.6% and front displacements of 0.3–0.8 mm are material for ML. A millimetre matters when a 1 mm fin occupies only two production cells. The effect also occurs without fins. The comparison file records a roughly 2.17 s snapshot mismatch for P0021, so matched-time interpolation and a consistent PCM/fin mask belong in a fine-reference evaluation. A common sign for melt time says nothing about the sign of a *model-ranking* change. [Stored spot checks](verification_data/spotcheck_pcm_stage0.json)

The pressure repair materially improves credibility. A terminal field can look plausible while a pressure solve repeatedly caps. The reported corrected-vs-original differences reach 8% in field temperature even when melt time changes little. Replacing failed cases, repairing 41 restarted series, and escalating the affected WARN category after P0075 crossed its threshold were proportionate responses (§11). The retained 39 WARN cases are reasonable conditional inclusions. Report results with and without them if they influence PCM conclusions. Equal file size is insufficient to establish identical contents; bind the accepted source path and hash to each case before training.

**Experimental scope.** In §6, the 3D Lou comparison measures a specific, meaningful observable: penetration of a buoyant charging jet. Across the eight printed times, the predicted-minus-PIV differences change from −8.5 mm at 150 s to +11.5 mm at 700 s. Thus the small reported 14-time mean bias conceals temporal structure. A maximum 18 mm difference between two PIV tests is not a pointwise uncertainty band. Lower-baffle refinement through 300 s checks one local mesh decision. These data support the 3D setup on that observable, with no direct validation of 2D jet mixing, discharge, the laminar regime, or the benchmark outlet temperature. The highest-value additional comparison is Lou’s outlet-temperature history using the existing 3D solution and the experimental measurement definition. It would connect the experiment to a quantity used in the benchmark without a new CFD campaign.

For Vogel, §10’s liquid-fraction deviation of 0.009 is against another **2D model**; the experimental deviation is 0.030. For Kamkari, the plain-wall 194.7 versus approximately 195 min endpoint is encouraging, but the history still differs by up to 0.054. For three fins, 115.5 and 121.3 versus approximately 125 min correspond to 7.6% and 3.0% early. Refinement improves this case. The residual +0.077 early liquid-fraction difference contains remaining discretization, properties, boundary conditions, experimental uncertainty, and modeling effects. Assigning it all to model error is unjustified. Mushy coefficients are material-specific, and their selection was informed by validation experience. Disclose which curves informed parameter choice and which configurations subsequently tested it. There is no scientific need to retune the coefficients to remove the three-fin discrepancy.

### What the ML evidence establishes

**Inputs and scores.** The run inputs are physically interpretable. ΔT normalization, dimensionless time, initial stratification, and a closure flag make sense for conditional emulation. The flag is essential when a design has two numerical targets. The PCM time scale is computed from input metadata, with an empirical heat-transfer coefficient; disclose its calibration provenance. Audit the evaluation horizon separately: requesting predictions only at times supplied by a true-melt-terminated file can leak the duration into a *melt-time evaluation*, even if the network never receives true melt time. Predict t99 by querying a predefined time grid and detecting the model’s own crossing. [PCM input code](thermbench_ml/pcm_data.py)

The tank scorer averages spatial RMS over snapshots. The energy score uses the difference in mean normalized temperature with constant \(\rho c_p\); production CFD uses variable properties. The outlet score uses the mean of port-adjacent cells, which can diverge from discharge-flux-weighted temperature during backflow. Thermocline error is conditional on both fields defining a crossing. Name these metrics precisely, publish the crossing-mismatch rate alongside midpoint error, and compare the energy and outlet proxies with archived physical series on the difficult cases. This matters more than another architecture. [Scoring code](thermbench_ml/metrics.py)

**Independence and generalization.** The split implementation protects closure twins from crossing partitions when their design metadata agree. Width extrapolation covers the tails of one rectangular geometry family. High-Re extrapolation contains only SST test cases, though SST is also present in training. Charge-only training tests transfer to discharge with a known approximate symmetry. Interpreting any of these as broad generalization to new tanks or physics would overstate the experiment. The canonical transform round-tripping accurately establishes its implementation’s reversibility. The transformation’s *physical* accuracy depends on symmetry of geometry, boundary and initial conditions, and properties. Temperature-dependent water properties break exact symmetry. [Split implementation](thermbench_ml/splits.py)

**Model comparison.** The strongest result in §8.4 is the factorial ablation on discharge. Plain FNO scores 64.8%, canonical alone 12.4%, hybrid alone 13.0%, and combined 4.48%. U-Net shows a similar progression to 4.37%. This supports using a known physical correspondence and a plug-flow prior together. A residual network can violate conservation even if its base conserves energy. The evidence also does not establish that successful discharge transfer is possible *only* through this transformation. On random cases the hybrid-canonical U-Net leads the corresponding FNO, 3.62% versus 4.02%; on unseen widths the FNO leads, 4.44% versus 6.23%. On high flow both weaken, and some U-Net entries are incomplete. Width performance on one native spacing does not demonstrate resolution independence. Present the architectures as co-equal baselines.

The 60-versus-100 epoch budgets can be fair if selected using validation convergence and accompanied by parameter counts, training compute, and comparable tuning effort. Section 7 says a “test pass” influenced the epoch choice, and the interim discharge results informed model development. Establish whether those design IDs recur in the final test partitions. Frozen split code protects against training overlap; it cannot undo development exposure. If overlap occurred, call those results development-informed and use a genuinely untouched confirmation set before making confirmatory architecture claims.

**Data efficiency.** From stored random-split scores, the hybrid-canonical versus plain FNO means are **1.82 versus 4.24 K at 5%**, **1.32 versus 2.12 K at 10%**, **1.17 versus 1.32 K at 25%**, and **1.07 versus 1.10 K at 50%**. The large advantage is concentrated at low sample counts. This is credible evidence for the *combined intervention on this split*. It does not identify which component saves data or establish the same benefit for OOD or PCM. Report unique training designs as well as run counts, whether subsets are nested and matched, and whether seeds vary initialization, subset draw, or both. [Stored learning-curve results](thermbench_ml/results_dataeff/random.json)

**Calibration and statistics.** Correlations of 0.82–0.94 indicate that spread ranks case difficulty well on these test sets. Some of that signal may distinguish low-Ri SST cases from easier cases. Within-regime correlations would show whether it also ranks cases of comparable difficulty. The random-split calibration file gives 90.84% coverage at nominal 90%. On discharge, §8.3 reports 71% at nominal 90%, worse than the 83% constant-bound comparator. High correlation and poor coverage coexist. Treat the spread as a calibrated indicator of *surrogate error against production CFD*. Three seeds are useful for baseline repeatability, modest for estimating seed variability, and insufficient for a general uncertainty guarantee. Use paired bootstrap intervals over physical designs, keeping closure twins together; show seed results separately and distinguish run-wise from cell-wise statistics. Cells and snapshots are not independent replicates. [Calibration results](thermbench_ml/capacity_2026-11-07/ml_calib_summary.json)

**Speed.** \(2.6\times3600/1.45\approx6{,}455\), so “about 6,000×” is numerically fair as a ratio of reported median wall times for a single-core CFD run and a GPU ensemble producing 120 snapshots. It is not a median paired speedup. State hardware, timing boundaries, preprocessing/I/O, and that training is amortized. This supports a latency claim for repeated inference. It does not quantify energy savings or equal-cost computational efficiency.

### Can the CFD reference change the ML conclusions?

**Yes for close rankings and absolute scores. The available evidence does not establish an actual reversal.** Let \(P\) be a fixed prediction, \(C\) the production reference, and \(F\) the alternative. On identical cells, times, weights and normalization, the change in a model’s RMS error is bounded by \(\|F-C\|\) by the triangle inequality. For two models, their score *gap* can change by up to \(2\|F-C\|\). The same reasoning applies to a mean of per-snapshot norms if the reference discrepancy is aggregated identically. The direction depends on how the model residual aligns with the reference change. A model that smooths a front may look better on a more diffusive grid.

Thus §5.1’s half-best-error rule is a useful engineering tolerance but mathematically insufficient to protect rankings. On T0211, for illustration, **if** the 7.9% ML error and 7.38% reference difference were computed with identical norms and samples, they would permit an alternate-reference error anywhere from 0.52% to 15.28%. Magnitudes alone cannot attribute any specific fraction to label error. The large plain-to-combined discharge improvement and the large low-data advantage are more convincing than the 0.11-point FNO/U-Net discharge difference. Their exact robustness still requires paired scoring. Outlet breakthrough, thermocline crossing and melt time can change sharply even when average energy is stable.

**Smallest decisive analysis:** freeze predictions and score both architectures on T0211 and T0052 against production and the existing alternate settings, plus available held-out fine-grid cases with at least one quiet control and one interface-sensitive case. Identify each model’s split membership; a training case can support a numerical diagnostic but cannot serve as OOD confirmation. Conservatively restrict fine fields to native cells, match physical times, use the same norm and masks, and report each model’s score and the paired gap on both references. Include event-window outlet error and front position. For PCM, repeat on available held-out members of the six fine checks once models exist, including t99 and front displacement. Report all available eligible cases to avoid selecting favorable outcomes. Keep flagged cases in the principal production score, with separate flagged-case and exclusion sensitivity results.

This test is **essential if the paper claims ranking robustness, physical accuracy at the scale of the ML errors, or useful fidelity beyond production labels**. For a narrowly stated fixed-reference benchmark it is an enhancement in principle. For this particular paper I would do the tank analysis before submission: the comparisons already exist, the two affected cases are test cases, and the answer could change the interpretation.

### Novelty and publication fit

The literature search changes the novelty framing. A 2025 *Energies* paper already predicts time-dependent 2D TES tank behavior from CFD-generated images using neural networks. PDEBench established public simulation datasets with FNO and U-Net comparisons in 2022, and The Well standardized surrogate tasks across diverse physical datasets in 2024. RP4 cannot claim novelty for neural prediction of tank fields or for pairing simulation datasets with standard architectures. [TES tank neural-network study](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/18/3/511) · [PDEBench](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/0a9747136d411fb83f0cf81820d44afb-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html) · [The Well](https://mlcommons-science.github.io/benchmark/md/benchmarks/the_well/)

Storage-specific prior art extends beyond the 320-case hydrate set. The latter publishes branched-fin parameters and performance metrics. An open finned shell-tube PCM dataset describes seven melting scenarios, and a February 2026 release provides experimental-validation-linked 3D enthalpy–porosity CFD data varying the mushy coefficient. These are material comparisons for §1. A focused search establishes relevant predecessors; it does not certify priority. [Hydrate surrogate dataset](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/69k294mhkh/1) · [Finned PCM dataset](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/7ht7jcpnjj/1) · [Mushy-coefficient dataset, 20 February 2026](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/5kvbfptt39/2)

The contribution with the strongest publication case is **two storage families with transient fields, fixed design-level OOD protocols, engineering scores, and released fidelity checks**. The hybrid residual method is a sensible baseline. The canonical-frame contribution is a storage-specific application with a strong ablation. The low-data finding has value precisely because its advantage shrinks with sample count. Multi-fidelity value currently lies in exposing where numerical targets move; a large multi-fidelity training resource remains prospective. *Journal of Energy Storage* is my first choice for the completed combined study. *Applied Thermal Engineering* becomes more attractive with the outlet comparison and stronger links to engineering observables. A computational-methods venue would need a more general methodological or fidelity result than the present architecture comparisons.

### A. Scientific verdict

ThermalStorageBench is a **credible numerical ML benchmark in development**. The tank family already supports meaningful comparisons against a defined 2D production solver. The combined study is publishable in scope, but the evidence presently supports neither dataset-wide discretization uncertainty bounds nor physical validation of all benchmark outputs. Alternate-reference results that preserve the large ablation effects would strengthen my assessment. A reversal of close architecture rankings would also be a valuable result if published transparently.

### B. Confirmed strengths

The most valuable design choice is the pairing of 192 physical designs across closures. It exposes modeling dependence at exactly the scale where surrogate errors are being interpreted. The project also retains numerical failures and corrected decisions in its record, uses physically intelligible OOD tests, and includes simple baselines capable of revealing when a neural model adds value. The discharge ablation and random-split low-data scores already answer concrete scientific questions.

### C. Most serious weaknesses

1. Sampled inter-grid differences have been promoted into uncertainty ceilings and ranking guarantees that the mathematics does not support.
2. The most sensitive tank test cases have ML errors comparable to their numerical-setting discrepancies, and the alternate-reference scores are missing.
3. Some engineering metric labels imply more physical fidelity than their implementations deliver.
4. Test-informed development, incomplete paired statistics, and unfinished PCM training limit confirmatory claims.
5. The two-family release, accepted-file identity check, and clean reproduction remain incomplete.

### D. Required versus optional work

### F. Publication recommendation

**Continue with the existing datasets, keep the two flagged tank cases visible, and delay the combined submission for the freeze, paired-reference analysis, final tank statistics, and PCM baselines.** I would fund the extraction of Lou’s outlet history before funding more meshes. I would change the rerun recommendation if the accepted-file audit uncovers wrong solver variants, if reference changes reverse an important conclusion across several independent designs, or if an identifiable input or boundary-condition defect recurs across a family. At present the evidence points to a stronger paper through better use of the simulations already available.
